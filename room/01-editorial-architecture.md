# Editorial architecture — first hypothesis

This is deliberately provisional.

## Home

Open with face, name, and a concise statement of the durable territory: systems, failure, intelligence, machines, and ideas worth making concrete. The first impression should be a person and a mind, not a product dashboard.

Below that, expose a wide, calm body of work: notes, series, projects, and a few connected paths into the archive. Recent activity can exist, but it should not define the identity.

## Possible public sections

- **About** — who this is, how the mind works, and what kind of problems attract attention.
- **Projects** — things being built or investigated with a concrete outcome, such as intelligent traffic lights.
- **Notes / Threads** — public-facing thinking, opinions, questions, and essays; less technical by default.
- **Technical** — tutorials and worked explanations, potentially replacing or renaming Bits2Bricks.
- **Learning** — only when a course, concept, book, or paper has become a durable piece of understanding.
- **Rabbit holes** — deep investigations sparked by a video, podcast, paper, or question, ending in an original synthesis or a small artefact when appropriate. This may be a format or series label rather than a top-level destination.
- **Second Brain** — the site's compact internal encyclopedia and connection layer, not the dominant homepage identity.
- **Activity** — a lightweight timeline of what has recently changed.
- **AMA** — a human entry point for questions.

The exact names are open. The important distinction is between:

1. exploration and synthesis (notes / rabbit holes);
2. explanation and application (technical work);
3. concrete making (projects);
4. the underlying connected archive (Second Brain).

## Dynamic idea

The strongest direction so far is a calm "current of ideas": content items reference one another across theory, practice, patterns, and projects. A series can be explicit, e.g. a data-centre capacity investigation with a sequence of linked posts. The movement should be editorial and spatial, not a constantly animated graph. A visitor should feel that one idea naturally leads to another.

## Production principle

The publishing system should make the path from conversation → sketch → explanation → finished post unusually short. A post may contain diagrams, rough visual reasoning, or AI-assisted illustrations without requiring the author to become a full-time illustrator.
