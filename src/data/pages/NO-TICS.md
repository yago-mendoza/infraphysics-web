# No tics

One paragraph, carried by every prompt that writes anything for this site or its second channel (articles, tweets, wikinotes, UI copy) and applied by any agent working in this repo before it shows a text. It is not style: it is the residue a model leaves and the author does not want to find, in either language. It sits here, beside [STYLE.md](STYLE.md), [VOICE.md](VOICE.md) and [SYNTAX.md](SYNTAX.md), because it applies to all of them at once; the long form for articles is STYLE.md (rules 1, 3, 9, 11) and this is the version short enough to sit in every prompt. `scripts/context-pack.js` reads the paragraph below from this file: it closes the prompt of every article pack in `_studio/articles/1-articles-format/` and is copied verbatim to `_studio/twitter/0-gen_prompts/_format_ctx/NO-TICS.md` for the hand-written tweet prompts. There is no second copy to keep in step.

Remove these from anything you write here before showing it, in English or Spanish: dashes as pauses (em-dash, en dash, double hyphen: a parenthesis, a period or a comma instead, chosen per phrase); the *not X, Y* reframe in all its moulds, also when split over two sentences, unless the contrast is itself the content (*it's not about speed, it's about trust*, *less like X and more like Y*, *X is not really about A. It is about B*, *this is not multitasking. It is continuous prospective memory*, *the problem is not that machines think. The problem is that humans stop noticing how they think*: say the thing); the provocative definition built the same way (*a modern developer is not only a programmer. He is also a biological scheduler*); the plain claim followed by its unexpected consequence (*we built machines to save time. Instead, they taught us to measure it more obsessively*); the causal inversion (*we think we are managing the tools. Perhaps the tools are training us*); the same syntactic opening repeated (*maybe… maybe… maybe…*, *some… some… some…*) and the ladder it builds (*first it is one build. Then ten agents. Then an entire working day organized around invisible countdowns*); the rhetorical question after a concrete observation, including the one that jumps to evolutionary or historical scale (*what happens when this becomes a permanent mode of thought?*, *humans evolved to remember where the fruit tree was. What happens when…*): if you have the answer, write it; the balanced antithesis in every shape it takes, which is one tic in different clothes, *it stops being X and starts being Y*, the second-person pivot (*you don't write the code any more; you review it*) and the two halves weighed on a semicolon (*aviation has checklists; software has code review*), where the symmetry is doing the work the content should be doing, and with it any continuous relation forced into X versus Y because it makes a clean sentence, when the real thing has degrees, exceptions and dependencies; the announced transition (*the scary part is*, *the real problem is*, *the interesting part is*, *the deeper point is*, *this changes everything because*, *here's where it gets interesting*): arrive at the idea directly and let it be scary on its own; the reinterpretation (*what this really means*, *in other words*, *this is really about*), which usually explains the same idea twice; the colon that sets up a timing clause (*the shift is simple: once you see it you cannot unsee it*); *funny how* as an opener; the analogy that hedges instead of committing (*weirdly similar to*, *oddly like*, *suspiciously close to*): either the two things are the same in a respect you can name, or the sentence has nothing; uncertainty words with no uncertainty behind them (*may*, *might*, *perhaps*, *maybe*, *I suspect*, *somewhat*), whether the sentence already knows the answer or the word is there to sound human; the trailing *y así*, *and so on*, *etc.*, which hands the reader a list you did not finish, and the list disguised as prose (*tests, latency, security, permissions*) when the items do not each matter; the mic-drop line after a wind-up, including the short sentence that lands a long one (*That's it.*, *…while silently remembering when each process should finish. Your brain keeps the clock.*); metacommentary on your own posture (*let me be direct*, *to be honest*); performed casualness (*vibes*, *rent-free*, *let that sink in*); the tricolon and the parallel run used for gravitas or rhythm (*we schedule meetings, we schedule software, and increasingly, we schedule our own attention*, *one agent writes the code, another reviews it, a third deploys it*); throat-clearing (*it's worth noting*, *furthermore*, *in summary*, *ultimately*); the hedged ending; decorated numbers (*an impressive 23%*); double quotes around a term (italics); arrows between concepts. Past the phrase, the same residue shows in how the thought moves, and it goes too: every paragraph turned into a thesis in the same clean mould of thesis, explanation and consequence under a logical hierarchy too perfect, when some sentences should only continue the idea, qualify it or describe a consequence, some are there for texture, tone or a hunch, and thought goes back, adds a precision late, finds an exception, connects something that looked secondary; rhetorical continuity where causal continuity belongs, a sentence arriving because a contrast, a metaphor, a conclusion or a new strong idea is due, when it should grow out of a consequence, a doubt or a problem the previous one left open; sentences optimised one by one, a closer every two sentences, lines designed to be quoted or to sound deep (*most branches disappear. A few survive.*, *more macrophage than operator*), every word chosen for effect, when a plain sentence is right if it moves the thought, some words only carry it, and long prose is not a collection of aphorisms; technical vocabulary used to raise the register and every concept perfectly named (*state space*, *admissible states*, *structure of the search*), when a specialised term earns its place by compressing a relation already made intuitive and otherwise mixes with the ordinary word; designed cadence, one sentence length throughout or short and long alternated mechanically, when some ideas need a long sentence because they carry conditions, others end at once, and a sentence may keep the irregularities of the thought (a late precision, an awkward subordinate clause, a correction on the way, a detour of half a second that comes back); the omniscient narrator who knows from the first line what everything means and what the text will prove, when a narrator can observe, suspect, correct itself and find a relation while writing, a sentence can start describing one thing and end understanding another, and uncertainty that really exists can stay unresolved (a real *in practice*, *at least in the limit*, a brief objection, a new idea whose edges are still blurred) without the paragraph having to look complete; the right name before the phenomenon, when people perceive something before they know what to call it: describe what is happening first and conceptualise after, let every category, dynamic or principle be born from something the reader can already picture (a process, a relation, a movement, an object, a situation), and bring formulas in late, once there is an intuition for them to compress, or the maths is decoration; a long stay at one level, only concepts or only examples, when the text should go down to something perceptible, back up to the general idea, and let each level explain the other; abstraction where the mechanism can be described (*the system becomes a search space*: say what happens to the branches, the dependencies and the states, and let the reader reach part of the conclusion), with the synthetic-essay connectors that carry it (*the codebase starts to look like…*, *development begins to resemble…*, *the same logic could…*); the concrete brought in as an example, announced with *for example* or chosen to represent its category perfectly (which reads as built from outside), when it should be what the narrator is thinking with, slightly arbitrary if need be (two agents editing the same parser, one breaking a migration while another fixes a test), and one well-chosen scene can carry causality, conflict, scale and uncertainty at once instead of a metaphor per concept; the metaphor arriving exactly where it is due and explained right after, when a writer would bring it in earlier, casually, even with some doubt, and leave part of its meaning implicit; and the conclusion before the mechanism, with the paragraph closed too well: if the reader can watch A produce B, do not write *therefore A causes B*, do not repeat as an aphorism what the paragraph already showed or spell out every causal link, stop one step short and leave the reader an inference. After each paragraph, look for structures that would survive swapping two nouns (*X is not really about A. It is about B.*) and rewrite most of them, and cut an explanatory sentence whose meaning already follows from what came before; after each sentence, ask what a person would think right after it, never what impressive sentence should come next. When a tic appears, rebuild the sentence or the paragraph around what it was trying to say; never swap one tic for another, and never remove specificity to add polish.
