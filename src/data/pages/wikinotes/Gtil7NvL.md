Tools are the workbench: editors, platforms, consoles, services, and automation used to inspect or change other systems. They are organized here by operational ownership rather than academic field.

- A tool earns its place by compressing a repeated decision or making hidden state observable. Feature count is a poor proxy for leverage.
- Every tool introduces a model of the work. When that model fits, it removes friction; when it does not, the user starts adapting the problem to the interface.
- [[oaFI1HMZ|Google]] and similar vendors are families of tools sharing identity and administration, not one coherent product.

The dangerous tool is not the complicated one. It is the convenient one whose defaults quietly become architecture.
