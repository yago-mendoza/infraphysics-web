**Original Equipment Manufacturer**: the maker of a specific machine or piece of equipment, compressors, turbines, pumps, packaging lines, robots, CNC machines.
- An OEM delivers a relatively autonomous unit: machine plus its own [[mfLejTTj|PLC]], sensors, actuators, [[NgpDKDPB|HMI]] and software. The machine then has to be integrated with the plant: OEM machine, its own controller, an integrator, the plant SCADA. That seam is where many interoperability problems are born ([[pHiCzY68|discrete automation]]).
- The OEM's back end is a business of its own: it buys components from [[Xu7qzqlL|automation vendors]], and after the sale it lives on spares, service and upgrades ([[o0FC5b14|aftermarket]]).
- An OEM with thousands of connected machines learns things no single plant can see, which makes it both a data source and a party in the negotiation over who owns machine data ([[hso3itYC|fleet data]], [[oWY2KCh3|data holders]]).

## Interactions

- [[cb9f4huK|system integrator]] : : The OEM optimizes one machine and ships it closed; the integrator has to open it enough to make it talk to everything else. The OEM's incentive is a self-contained product, the integrator's is interoperability, and the plant pays for the gap between them
