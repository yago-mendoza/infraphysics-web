Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) centralizes logs and security events so they can be searched, correlated, retained and turned into alerts. Its raw material is evidence produced by many other systems.

A SIEM can detect nothing useful if telemetry is missing, identifiers do not line up or rules drown analysts in noise. Collection is not understanding. The engineering work lies in normalization, correlation, detection logic and an operational response path.

A warehouse full of clues is valuable only if somebody can reconstruct the incident.
