The companies around a plant and how money and data flow between them. A plant is not only a process and an information flow; it is a capital flow that keeps it alive, and studying PLCs without knowing who buys from whom misses half the system ([[mckHgOqe|industrial automation]]).

{bkqt/keyconcept|The actors}
EPC: designs, procures, builds and commissions a large plant, then hands it over.
Automation vendor: sells PLCs, DCS, HMI, SCADA, drives, I/O, networking and software (Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, ABB, Emerson, Honeywell, Yokogawa).
System integrator: joins equipment of different brands, programs, configures communications, builds HMI and SCADA, commissions, and often stays under a maintenance contract.
OEM: makes a specific machine (compressor, packaging line, robot, CNC) and delivers it with its own controller and software.
The plant: purchasing, production and maintenance departments, with high technical knowledge.
{/bkqt}

- There is no single commercial chain. A plant owner may buy through an [[HHIC3rhB|EPC]], directly from an [[Xu7qzqlL|automation vendor]], through a [[cb9f4huK|system integrator]], or receive an [[pC4MKpUX|OEM]] machine that someone then has to integrate. Each path ends at the same [[rNOGsjVE|sophisticated buyer]].
- After the plant is built, the long business starts: the [[o0FC5b14|aftermarket]] of spares, service, software and upgrades over fifteen to thirty years, and the newest and tensest negotiation, who holds the machine's data ([[oWY2KCh3|data holders]]).
