An **ordinary differential equation** has one independent variable, almost always [[0q8Le7Y9|time]], and unknowns that are functions of it. Everything that varies does so along one axis, and the derivatives are total derivatives.
- The general form used across these notes is a system of first-order equations, \(\dot x=f(x,u,t)\), with \(x\) a vector. A higher-order equation is the same object after renaming derivatives as states: a second-order equation becomes two first-order ones by introducing the velocity, as the [[kbpURjgr|oscillator]] shows.
- Whether \(t\) appears in \(f\) splits the family into [[z2Ozbisa|autonomous]] systems, whose field is fixed, and non-autonomous ones, whose field moves as time passes.
- Its solutions are trajectories through a state space, one per [[wwPZUijW|initial condition]]; the geometry of all of them is the [[PeYZGshp|phase portrait]]. Where the unknown depends on space as well, the equation is a [[ucNBFjoJ|PDE]].
