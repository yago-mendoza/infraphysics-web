The companies that sell the components of automation: [[mfLejTTj|PLC]], [[7NrcWZUv|DCS]], [[NgpDKDPB|HMI]], [[es6Sv4Zb|SCADA]], drives ([[keWFUA5P|VFD]]), remote I/O, instrumentation, networking and software. Siemens, Rockwell Automation, Schneider Electric, ABB, Emerson, Honeywell, Yokogawa.
- Their customers are plants, [[HHIC3rhB|EPCs]], [[cb9f4huK|integrators]] and [[pC4MKpUX|OEMs]], through any of the commercial chains ([[nLtOMdEo|industrial ecosystem]]).
- Each big vendor owns an ecosystem: its own industrial Ethernet (PROFINET for Siemens, EtherNet/IP for Rockwell, CC-Link IE for Mitsubishi), its own engineering tools, its own DCS platform. Choosing a vendor is choosing a network, a toolchain and the integrators who know them ([[1pkzK8nT|industrial Ethernet]]).
- The strategic incentive follows: the more integrated a plant is with one vendor's software, the higher its switching cost, and the longer the vendor earns on the [[o0FC5b14|aftermarket]].

## Interactions

- [[rNOGsjVE|sophisticated buyer]] : : The vendor sells an ecosystem and the lock-in that comes with it; the buyer knows exactly what leaving would cost and prices that into every negotiation. Neither side is naive, which is why industrial deals are slow and hard
