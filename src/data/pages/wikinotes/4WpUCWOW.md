Brains are adaptive control systems built from noisy biological components. They connect cellular dynamics, learning, perception, memory, action, and subjective experience across scales that are individually understandable but collectively unresolved.

- A neuron is not a transistor and a brain is not merely a larger neural network. Timing, chemistry, embodiment, recurrent dynamics, and continuous adaptation matter.
- [[7aLJOACt|Machine learning]] borrows vocabulary from neuroscience more readily than mechanisms. The analogy can generate hypotheses, but it can also smuggle confidence across a gap in evidence.
- [[rttI47hN|Systems theory]] is useful because cognition is not located in a single component; it emerges from interactions, feedback, constraints, and environment.

The surprising fact is not that brains sometimes fail. It is that unreliable parts produce coherent behavior at all.
