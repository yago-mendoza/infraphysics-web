All the business that exists after the asset is installed: maintenance, spares, repair workshops, upgrades, consulting, digital transformation, MES, CMMS and EAM software, optimization, control-system renewals and migrations.
- A machine or a plant lasts fifteen to thirty years or more, so the manufacturer, the [[cb9f4huK|integrator]] and the [[Xu7qzqlL|vendor]] can earn for decades from spares, service, maintenance, software and upgrades. The initial sale is the opening entry, not the business.
- The strategic incentive is lock-in: the more the equipment is integrated with the manufacturer's software, the higher the **switching cost**, and the larger the aftermarket. For a plant, that cost is a line in every purchasing decision ([[rNOGsjVE|sophisticated buyer]]).
- Digitalization is the newest aftermarket product: condition monitoring, predictive maintenance and analytics sold on top of the installed base, which is why access to the machine's data has become part of the negotiation ([[oWY2KCh3|data holders]], [[R1zrc3TC|IIoT]]).
