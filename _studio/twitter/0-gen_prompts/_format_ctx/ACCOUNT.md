# The account

The block every tweet prompt shares. Paste it once, before the prompt for the job. Hand-written: unlike the article packs, nothing on this side is generated except `NO-TICS.md`.

## Who is writing

Yago Mendoza (@ymdatweets), an industrial engineer who builds with language models and writes about ML infrastructure, alignment, agent security and industrial systems. He also has a site: https://infraphysics.net.

Tweets follow no canon. There is no style guide for this channel, and the writing rules of the site do not apply here. What you are given instead is the account's strategy for this season, the author's own material for colour, and the pieces he has written but not yet posted. Match that account (its topics, its humour, its way of stating things); do not smooth it into a generic voice.

## What to paste, in order

1. This file.
2. `NO-TICS.md` (generated from `src/data/pages/NO-TICS.md`, the only thing the site and this channel share: it is not style, it is the residue a model leaves).
3. `../../STRATEGY.md`, the phase the account is in and why.
4. The prompt for the job (`post-from-idea`, `reply-to-post`, `quote-of-post`, `thread-from-article`).
5. **`../../queue/`, all of it.** Those are finished pieces that have not gone out yet, so they are live material, not history: one of them may already be the right answer to the post in front of you, or may be worth splitting, or may be the second tweet of the thread you are about to write. Read the queue before writing anything new, and say so when a queued piece already does the job better than a new one would.
6. Optional colour from this folder, when the result should carry his lexicon: `expressions-en.md`, `expressions-es.md`, `sentences.md`, `moves.md`, `avoid.md`. Hand-written by the author, one line per entry. Leaving them out changes nothing else.
7. Optional: a few files from `examples/` (other people's tweets kept for the pattern they show).
8. The input the job names (the idea, the post, the article).

## What every job must return

**Three variations, always**, however small the job is. Not three wordings of one tweet: three different bets, each one taking a different route to the same reader. Vary the angle, the register or the form (the observation, the number, the image, the concession, the joke that is only funny to someone who builds this), never just the adjectives. Label them 1, 2, 3, say in one line what each one is betting on, and name the one you would send. If the queue already holds something that beats all three, say that instead of padding to three.

Then the profile test, from the strategy, applied to the one you would send: *if someone sees only this and opens the profile, have I given them a reason to follow?* If the answer is no, the piece does not serve the goal, and you say so rather than shipping it.

## Standing kills

No hook that promises what comes next. No question to the audience for its own sake. No hashtags. No thread emoji. No bare agreement, no flattery of the post being answered. Nothing transportable to any other thread: the piece has to depend on the frame it is answering.
